# Annotated source library

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-19

This library prioritizes peer-reviewed papers, primary research, meta-analyses, and authoritative standards. A source’s inclusion does not mean every claim in it has been independently replicated.

## Reading rate and RSVP

1. **Rayner, K., Schotter, E. R., Masson, M. E. J., Potter, M. C., & Treiman, R. (2016). _So Much to Read, So Little Time: How Do We Read, and Can Speed Reading Help?_** Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 17(1), 4–34. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100615623267)  
   Major review of normal reading, speed reading, RSVP, skimming, and the speed–accuracy tradeoff. Core source for rejecting extreme comprehension-preserving speed claims.

2. **Brysbaert, M. (2019). _How many words do we read per minute? A review and meta-analysis of reading rate._** Journal of Memory and Language, 109, 104047. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2019.104047) · [University record](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/8647789)  
   Meta-analysis of 190 studies and 18,573 participants; estimates 238 wpm for English nonfiction and 260 wpm for fiction on average.

3. **Benedetto, S., Carbone, A., Pedrotti, M., Le Fevre, K., Yahia Bey, L. A., & Baccino, T. (2015). _Rapid serial visual presentation in reading: The case of Spritz._** Computers in Human Behavior, 45, 352–358. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2014.12.043)  
   Direct comparison reporting impaired literal comprehension and greater visual fatigue for Spritz relative to traditional reading.

4. **Acklin, D., & Papesh, M. H. (2017). _Modern Speed-Reading Apps Do Not Foster Reading Comprehension._** American Journal of Psychology, 130(2), 183–199. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5406/amerjpsyc.130.2.0183) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29461715/)  
   Compares static text with RSVP at 700 and 1,000 wpm and varies text difficulty. Static text produced superior overall comprehension.

5. **Proaps, A. B., & Bliss, J. P. (2014). _The effects of text presentation format on reading comprehension and video game performance._** Computers in Human Behavior, 36, 41–47. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2014.03.039)  
   Applied comparison of RSVP/traditional presentation and sentence/content-word formats. Useful evidence that engagement and challenge can diverge from comprehension.

6. **Primativo, S., Spinelli, D., Zoccolotti, P., De Luca, M., & Martelli, M. (2016). _Perceptual and cognitive factors imposing “speed limits” on reading rate: a study with the rapid serial visual presentation._** PLOS ONE, 11(4), e0153786. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0153786)  
   Separates decoding, memory span, semantic context, masking, and eye-movement contributions across RSVP experiments. Important caution: fast decoding or recognition is not passage comprehension.

7. **Pratt, E., & Fernández, E. M. (2016). _Implicit Prosody and Cue-based Retrieval: L1 and L2 Agreement and Comprehension during Reading._** Frontiers in Psychology, 7, 1922. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5156782/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01922)  
   Compares sentence, word-by-word RSVP, and phrase presentation, showing that presentation format interacts with language background and processing. Supports phrase-aware design and warns against one-size-fits-all claims.

8. **Folk, J. R., & Eskenazi, M. A. (2018). _Eye-tracking to distinguish comprehension-based and oculomotor-based regressive eye movements during reading._** Journal of Visualized Experiments, 140, e58442. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6235565/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3791/58442)  
   Methodological evidence that regressions have different causes, including comprehension difficulty; reinforces the need for repair rather than simply suppressing backward movement.

## Rapid visual processing and memory

9. **Potter, M. C., Wyble, B., Hagmann, C. E., & McCourt, E. S. (2014). _Detecting meaning in RSVP at 13 ms per picture._** Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, 76, 270–279. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-013-0605-z) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24374558/)  
   Demonstrates exceptionally rapid above-chance scene target detection under laboratory conditions. Supports fast gist, not fast durable learning.

10. **Potter, M. C., & Fox, L. F. (2009). _Detecting and remembering simultaneous pictures in a rapid serial visual presentation._** Perception & Psychophysics, 71, 989–1002. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2696395/)  
    Directly contrasts target detection with recognition memory. Recognition was much weaker, and blank consolidation time mattered.

11. **Potter, M. C. (2012). _Recognition and memory for briefly presented scenes._** Frontiers in Psychology, 3, 32. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284209/)  
    Review of the distinction between rapid conceptual access and memory consolidation; “momentary comprehension” is not a guarantee of later memory.

12. **Dux, P. E., & Marois, R. (2009). _How humans search for targets through time: A review of data and theory from the attentional blink._** Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, 71, 1683–1700. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2915904/)  
    Reviews the temporal competition that makes a second target difficult to report shortly after the first.

## Multimedia, pacing, and active learning

13. **Rey, G. D., Beege, M., Nebel, S., Wirzberger, M., Schmitt, T. H., & Schneider, S. (2019). _A meta-analysis of the segmenting effect._** Educational Psychology Review, 31, 389–419. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4)  
    Meta-analysis supporting segmented multimedia for retention, transfer, and cognitive load across many studies, with boundary conditions and greater learning time.

14. **Kühl, T., Eitel, A., Damnik, G., & Körndle, H. (2014). _The impact of disfluency, pacing, and students’ need for cognition on learning with multimedia._** Computers in Human Behavior, 35, 189–198. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2014.03.004)  
    Self-pacing increased learning time and improved retention/transfer in the study; illustrates that time and understanding must be measured together.

15. **Wang, F., Zhao, T., Mayer, R. E., & Wang, Y. (2021). _Guiding the learner’s cognitive processing of a narrated animation._** Learning and Instruction, 71, 101357. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2020.101357)  
    Three experiments on eye-movement modeling cues. Cues guided attention and improved outcomes in tested conditions, with pacing as a boundary condition.

16. **Szpunar, K. K., Khan, N. Y., & Schacter, D. L. (2013). _Interpolated memory tests reduce mind wandering and improve learning of online lectures._** PNAS, 110(16), 6313–6317. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1221764110)  
    Two experiments showing benefits of short interpolated tests for attention, note-taking, and learning in online lectures.

17. **McDermott, K. B. (2021). _Practicing Retrieval Facilitates Learning._** Annual Review of Psychology, 72, 609–633. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33006925/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010419-051019)  
    Broad review of retrieval practice across materials, ages, learner abilities, and classroom contexts.

18. **Mawson, R. D., & Kang, S. H. K. (2025). _The Distributed Practice Effect on Classroom Learning: A Meta-Analytic Review of Applied Research._** Behavioral Sciences, 15(6), 771. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12189222/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs15060771)  
    Classroom-focused meta-analysis of 22 reports/31 effects, finding a moderate benefit for distributed practice with substantial context dependence.

19. **Dignath, C., van Ewijk, R., Perels, F., & Fabriz, S. (2023). _Let Learners Monitor the Learning Content and Their Learning Behavior!_** Educational Psychology Review, 35, 62. [Full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09718-4)  
    Meta-analysis of monitoring tools. Supports content-focused monitoring while documenting substantial heterogeneity and implementation effects.

20. **Bobek, E., & Tversky, B. (2016). _Creating visual explanations improves learning._** Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications, 1, 27. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5256450/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-016-0031-6)  
    Evidence for learner-generated visual explanations in tested science-learning contexts; supports bounded generative frames.

## Adaptive and frontier interfaces

21. **Kosch, T., Schmidt, A., Thanheiser, S., & Chuang, L. L. (2020). _One Does Not Simply RSVP: Mental Workload to Select Speed Reading Parameters Using Electroencephalography._** CHI 2020. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376766) · [Author PDF](https://hcistudio.org/publication/kosch2020one/kosch2020one.pdf)  
    EEG measures tracked gains in reading speed more reliably than comprehension or subjective workload. Useful proof of concept and warning against proxy optimization.

22. **Andreessen, L. M., Gerjets, P., Meurers, D., & Zander, T. O. (2021). _Toward neuroadaptive support technologies for improving digital reading._** User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction, 31, 75–104. [Full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11257-020-09273-5) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-020-09273-5)  
    Small subject-specific EEG study distinguishing workload associated with text difficulty and presentation speed. Interesting research layer, not an MVP dependency.

23. **Grootjen, M. et al. (2024). _Your Eyes on Speed: Using Pupil Dilation to Adaptively Select Speed-Reading Parameters in Virtual Reality._** Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction, 8, Article 284. [Author PDF](https://hcistudio.org/publication/grootjen2024your/grootjen2024your.pdf)  
    Explores pupil dilation, task load, comprehension, and RSVP speed in VR. The authors emphasize lighting and individual-difference challenges.

24. **van der Sluis, F., & van den Broek, E. L. (2023). _Feedback beyond accuracy: Using eye-tracking to detect comprehensibility and interest during reading._** Journal of the Association for Information Science and Technology, 74(1), 3–16. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10084433/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.24657)  
    Thirty-participant study in which eye behavior explained variance in subjective comprehensibility and interest. Supports implicit-feedback research, not direct comprehension inference.

25. **Säuberli, A., Jepifanova, D., Frassinelli, D., & Plank, B. (2026). _Controlling Reading Ease with Gaze-Guided Text Generation._** arXiv preprint. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17781)  
    **Experimental/preprint.** Uses a predicted gaze metric to guide text generation and evaluates generated text with eye tracking. Concerns reading ease, not source-fidelity or durable learning.

26. **Zhuang, H. et al. (2025). _Detecting Reading-Induced Confusion Using EEG and Eye Tracking._** arXiv preprint. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14442)  
    **Preliminary/preprint, n=11.** Multimodal confusion classification suggests a frontier direction but is far from consumer validation.

## Accessibility standard

27. **W3C. _Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2._** [Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) · [Understanding seizures and physical reactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/seizures-and-physical-reactions.html)  
    Authoritative requirements and guidance for flashing, animation, operability, and alternatives. PRISM should adopt a stricter no-full-field-flash product rule.

## Long-document and PDF intelligence

28. **Liu, N. F., Lin, K., Hewitt, J., Paranjape, A., Bevilacqua, M., Petroni, F., & Liang, P. (2024). _Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts._** Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics, 12, 157–173. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00638) · [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)  
    Shows strong position effects in studied long-context retrieval and QA settings. Supports explicit hierarchical retrieval rather than treating a full context window as reliable memory.

29. **Ouyang, L. et al. (2025). _OmniDocBench: Benchmarking Diverse PDF Document Parsing with Comprehensive Annotations._** CVPR 2025, 24838–24848. [CVF paper page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Ouyang_OmniDocBench_Benchmarking_Diverse_PDF_Document_Parsing_with_Comprehensive_Annotations_CVPR_2025_paper.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR52734.2025.02313)  
    Peer-reviewed document-parsing benchmark spanning academic papers, textbooks, slides, newspapers, handwritten notes, and other layouts. Reinforces the need for element-specific extraction metrics and a representative golden corpus.

30. **Chia, Y. K., Cheng, L., Chan, H. P., Song, M., Liu, C., Aljunied, M., Poria, S., & Bing, L. (2025). _M-LongDoc: A Benchmark For Multimodal Super-Long Document Understanding And A Retrieval-Aware Tuning Framework._** EMNLP 2025, 9233–9250. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.469/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.469)  
    Benchmark with documents averaging hundreds of pages and open-ended explanations over text, figures, and tables. Reports modality and irrelevant-context challenges, supporting page/element retrieval and separate visual fidelity checks.

31. **Deep Search Team. (2024). _Docling Technical Report._** arXiv preprint. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09869) · [Project](https://github.com/docling-project/docling)  
    **Engineering preprint.** Describes an MIT-licensed local document-conversion pipeline using layout and table-structure models. It motivates a parser candidate, not a claim that Docling is uniformly best for PRISM’s corpus.

32. **Adhikari, N. S., & Agarwal, S. (2024). _A Comparative Study of PDF Parsing Tools Across Diverse Document Categories._** arXiv preprint. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09871)  
    **Engineering preprint.** Reports substantial parser differences by document class and continued difficulty with scientific and patent documents. Supports routing by page/document type and evaluating on PRISM’s own fixtures.
